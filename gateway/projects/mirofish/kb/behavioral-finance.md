# Behavioral Finance Heuristics

## Key Biases to Model

### Anchoring
People fixate on the first piece of information they encounter. If a market opens at 50%, participants anchor to that number even as new information arrives.

### Herding
Individuals follow the crowd, especially under uncertainty. Social proof drives momentum — if "everyone" thinks X, late participants adopt X without independent analysis.

### Loss Aversion
Losses feel ~2x worse than equivalent gains (Kahneman & Tversky). Agents will hold losing positions too long and sell winners too early. This creates predictable price asymmetries.

### Recency Bias
Recent events are weighted disproportionately. The last data point dominates mental models even when long-term trends tell a different story.

### Overconfidence
Experts consistently overestimate their predictive accuracy. Domain specialists are particularly prone — they know more but calibrate worse than generalists.

### Availability Heuristic
Events that are easy to recall (dramatic, recent, personal) are judged as more likely. Media coverage directly inflates perceived probability.

### Confirmation Bias
Agents seek and interpret information that confirms their existing beliefs. Once a position is taken, contrary evidence is discounted.

## Crowd Dynamics

### Information Cascades
Early movers set the narrative. If the first few vocal agents agree, later agents follow regardless of private information. Cascades are fragile — a single credible dissenter can break them.

### Polarization
Group discussion pushes opinions toward extremes. Moderate initial views become stronger after debate. Prediction markets exhibit this when trading volume increases.

### Wisdom of Crowds Conditions
Crowds are wise when: diverse opinions, independent judgment, decentralized information, mechanism for aggregation. Remove any condition and crowd accuracy degrades.
