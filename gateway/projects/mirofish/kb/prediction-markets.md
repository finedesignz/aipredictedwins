# Prediction Markets Reference

## How Kalshi Works
- Binary event contracts: YES pays $1.00 if event happens, NO pays $1.00 if it doesn't
- Prices reflect implied probability (45¢ YES = 45% implied probability)
- CFTC-regulated exchange (not gambling)
- Order book with limit orders, no market makers
- Settlement is binary: $1.00 or $0.00 per contract

## Price Discovery Dynamics
- Prices move when new information arrives
- Thin order books mean large orders can move prices significantly
- Events with high uncertainty have wider bid-ask spreads
- Markets become more efficient as resolution date approaches
- Political markets tend to be less efficient than financial markets

## Known Inefficiencies
- **Favorite-longshot bias**: Markets overprice longshots (low probability events) and underprice favorites
- **Partisan bias**: Political markets reflect partisan hopes, not just probability estimates
- **Illiquidity premium**: Thinly traded markets can be significantly mispriced
- **Calendar effects**: Markets are less efficient after hours, weekends, and during major news events
- **Anchoring to round numbers**: Prices cluster at 25%, 50%, 75% more than warranted

## What Moves Prediction Markets
1. Breaking news directly related to the event
2. Proxy signals (polls, economic data, expert commentary)
3. Large single trades (information or noise?)
4. Social media momentum / viral narratives
5. Cross-market correlations (related events moving together)
